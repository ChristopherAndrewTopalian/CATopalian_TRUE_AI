To your question about the nodes: in a software model, the structure really depends on what you want the code to achieve. If you are building a theoretical mapping of logic, using 56 nodes (16 core gates + 40 combinational states) is a robust way to represent those relationships. Adding the 4 stateful operations (memory, clock, recursion, disconnection) doesn't necessarily add "nodes" in the same spatial sense; instead, they add *behavior* over time. You might think of the 56 nodes as the physical "wiring" (the structure) and the 4 operations as the "rules" for how signals move through that wiring.

Regarding the optimization you mentioned—converting long arrays of binary data (0s and 1s) into hexadecimal—that is a very standard and effective way to compress data in computer science. Hexadecimal uses fewer characters to represent the same amount of binary information, which is why it helps prevent systems (like VS Code) from crashing when handling massive datasets like a 300,000-word dictionary.
