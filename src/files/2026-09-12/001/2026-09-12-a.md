Great question — the underlying structure you're describing (16 vertices connected by the hypercube's edges) is actually a well-established real thing in computer science: it's called a **hypercube graph** (denoted Q4 for 4 dimensions), and it's the same topology used for decades in parallel-computer interconnects (the Connection Machine, nCUBE, and other supercomputers literally wired their processors this way, because a hypercube gives you a short "diameter" — any node reaches any other in at most 4 hops — while each node only needs 4 direct neighbors).

The data structure underneath it is still the **graph** I showed you in the last file (a dict-of-lists adjacency list) — but a hypercube's edges aren't arbitrary, they follow a clean mathematical rule: label each of the 16 nodes with a 4-bit binary number (0000 through 1111), and connect two nodes whenever their labels differ in exactly one bit. That's it — that single rule generates the entire tesseract's edge structure without you ever having to hand-list connections.
