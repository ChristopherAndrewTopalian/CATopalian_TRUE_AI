Here's the key insight that fixes it, and it resolves your "center node" question at the same time: the 16-function ceiling isn't really about depth — it's about only having **2 raw input bits** (`a` and `b`). No amount of stacking gates on the same two bits escapes 2⁴=16 possible outcomes. But your tesseract already has the answer sitting right in it: **4 dimensions, 4 independent bits.** If you feed the network 4 raw inputs instead of 2, and use layers of your 2-input gates to combine them, the space of representable functions jumps to 2^(2⁴) = **65,536** distinct possibilities — because now there are 16 possible input rows to assign outputs to, not 4. That's the real scaling mechanism: more independent inputs, combined through layers, using the same 16 gates you already built.
